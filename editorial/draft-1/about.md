# About Me

I work on what happens when computing grows beyond the individual machine.

I'm Vijoy Pandey, an engineer and technology leader. I lead Outshift by Cisco, its incubation engine for emerging technologies. My work spans agentic AI, distributed systems, quantum networking and emerging forms of physical and embodied computation.

Across these subjects I keep returning to the same architectural question: what needs to be shared, preserved or coordinated when increasingly capable systems must work together?

At Outshift, that question has led to work on open agent interoperability, semantic coordination and quantum networking systems. Outside the day-to-day work of building, I write and host Coherent Cognition, where I explore research, ideas and people shaping the next landscape of computing.

I hold a Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of California, Davis, and have filed more than 80 patents. Those are pieces of a professional history, not the reason this site exists.

This site is a place to follow ideas as they evolve: my own work, the work of researchers I learn from, and questions for which I don't yet have answers.

# How I Work
## Curiosity is useful only when it changes what we do.

I enjoy the distance between an interesting idea and something that works in the world. Crossing it requires curiosity, experimentation, judgment and often an uncomfortable willingness to abandon approaches that once seemed obvious.

Four operating principles guide much of my work: feed your curiosity, act with urgency, embrace challenges, and make it real. They are not slogans about moving faster for its own sake. Curiosity broadens the options. Urgency shortens learning loops. Ambitious problems force new methods. Reality provides the final test.

AI is changing the economics of execution. Small teams equipped with capable agent systems can attempt work that would once have required larger organizations. But local productivity is not the same as organizational productivity. Tiny Teams with Tokens—T3—makes the productive unit smaller while potentially expanding the coordination surface between teams.

Three things must therefore evolve together: the unit of productivity, the context that preserves decisions and authority, and the structures through which teams coordinate. Faster teams without shared context simply diverge faster. A shared knowledge base without clear decision rights does not solve the problem either.

That changes what I value in leaders and collaborators. Judgment matters when the ability to execute becomes abundant. Influence matters when work crosses boundaries no reporting line can resolve. Accountability matters because delegating tasks or using agents never delegates responsibility for outcomes.

I prefer centralizing shared foundations—security, interoperability, context and evaluation—while letting small teams own domain decisions and experiments. Measure what becomes possible, what reaches users, what is reliable, and what matters; not simply activity, tokens or lines of code.

The connection to the technical thesis is deliberate. Making each node stronger is insufficient if the system cannot coordinate. The same can be true of organizations.
