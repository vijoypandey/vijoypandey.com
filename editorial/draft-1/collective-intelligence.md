# Collective Intelligence
## What happens when intelligence becomes collective?

We have learned to make individual AI systems remarkably capable. We are also getting better at connecting them through tools, messages and interoperable protocols. Yet connection alone does not make a group intelligent.

An agent may understand its assignment, produce a defensible output and communicate flawlessly. Another can do the same. Together they may still reach the wrong answer. The failure may lie not in transport, authentication or execution, but in meanings that drift, assumptions never reconciled and objectives that no longer align.

Think about a collection of specialized agents handling a customer commitment. Sales hears one requirement, engineering another, legal translates it into a contract, and finance evaluates different constraints. Messages arrive successfully. Locally reasonable decisions compound into a collectively wrong result. More agents can amplify—not cure—the mismatch.

This suggests a distinction between the Internet of Agents and an Internet of Cognition. Discovery, identity, communication and access control are essential foundations. Beyond them lies a layer of coordination concerned with shared intent, context, authority, and the ability to challenge or verify conclusions. Calling this a 'layer' is an architectural proposal, not a claim that the global Internet already has a standardized ninth layer.

I find the human analogy provocative: language helps us accumulate shared knowledge, align purpose, and reason with other people. But human collectives also display groupthink and confident error. Consensus is not equivalent to correctness. The question is how artificial collectives develop both the ability to collaborate and the discipline to disagree.

Research into silent failures, semantic consistency and coordination protocols offers concrete ways to investigate the problem. Observability that reports healthy workflows is insufficient if the final decision is unsound. Better collective systems may need to expose their disagreements, establish explicit intent contracts and verify outcomes against the world.

### Questions worth pursuing
- How can autonomous agents discover that they use the same words differently?
- How do shared context and authority persist across organizations and time?
- When does debate improve a collective answer—and when does it simply manufacture consensus?
- What can be verified deterministically, and what remains a probabilistic judgment?
- How does coordination scale as specialized agent teams become more autonomous?
