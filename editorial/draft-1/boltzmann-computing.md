# Boltzmann Computing
## What happens when matter itself computes?

Digital computing is an extraordinary achievement. It gives us reliable, composable operations built from discrete states. But not every computational problem maps naturally onto an explicit sequence of digital instructions.

For optimization, sampling and inference, the structure of a problem may be expressed through an energy landscape or a probability distribution. This raises a different possibility: rather than digitally simulating every transition, can we engineer a physical system whose own dynamics do useful computational work?

Thermodynamic systems may exploit stochastic fluctuations and relaxation. Networks of coupled oscillators may encode solutions through phase relationships and synchronization. These are related explorations of physics-native computation, but they are not interchangeable: an oscillator system does not necessarily sample a Boltzmann distribution.

The attractive idea is that useful answers emerge from the dynamics of the substrate. The difficult questions concern accuracy, controllability, energy accounting, noise, calibration, and competition with highly optimized digital methods. Demonstrating a striking physical effect is not the same as demonstrating a practical computational advantage.

And what would horizontal scale mean? When two chips rely on correlated fluctuations or phase relationships, dividing a problem across them may change the underlying dynamics. Sometimes coupling is the computation itself. Sometimes a classical network or a hybrid architecture may suffice. We should investigate rather than assume.

This is an emerging reading and research frontier for me. I am especially interested in work that explains what the physical system actually computes, the conditions under which it does so, and the limits of scaling it.

### Questions worth pursuing
- Which workloads benefit from physical stochastic dynamics rather than digital simulation?
- How should performance, energy and solution quality be compared fairly?
- When are phase, coupling and correlations computational state rather than implementation detail?
- Can many small physical engines solve a problem as one coherent system?
- Where do thermodynamic, oscillator and quantum approaches genuinely differ?
