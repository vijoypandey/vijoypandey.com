# Homepage — Beyond the Bit

## Thesis
### Beyond the Bit: What Should the Network Carry Next?

We invented the bit. We learned to distribute bits. We are now computing with richer forms of state. The network must evolve.

Computing scales through better algorithms, more powerful machines, and connected machines working together. Networks made the third possible by moving data between billions of endpoints. But the endpoints themselves are changing. They think, entangle, fluctuate, and move.

The question I keep returning to is simple: what must survive the boundary between systems for them to accomplish something together that neither could accomplish alone?

The answers are different. Intelligent agents need shared intent and context. Quantum systems must establish and use entanglement without copying arbitrary quantum states. Physical machines need sensing and action coordinated in space and time. Thermodynamic and oscillator systems raise questions about correlations and dynamics across coupled systems.

This is an exploration of those boundaries, the science behind them, and the architectures we may need next.

## Four frontiers
### Collective Intelligence
Connecting intelligent agents is not the same as enabling them to think together. What changes when intelligence becomes collective?

### Quantum Computing
Quantum processors are gaining capability. What does it take to extend quantum computation, sensing and entanglement beyond one device?

### Physical AI
Intelligence enters a world that moves even when software is waiting. How do machines perceive, coordinate and act together safely?

### Boltzmann Computing
What if physical dynamics are not noise to suppress, but resources for computation? And can such systems scale through coupling?
